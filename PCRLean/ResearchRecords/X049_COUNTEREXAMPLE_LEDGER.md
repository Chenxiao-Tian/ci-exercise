# X049 Counterexample and Failure-Boundary Ledger

## 1. An upper regular centre need not descend

For `A=k[t]`, `B=k[s]`, `t=s^e`, the ideal `(s)` is regular upstairs but is not extended from `A`. Blowing up `(t)` is the identity. Regularity of the upper centre does not imply eventual descendability.

## 2. Divisorial residue cannot be killed by codimension-two preparation

If the exponent vector above a base divisor is not in the ramification lattice, every proper birational base modification is an isomorphism at the old divisor's generic point. The residue persists. The balanced-gap flatification theorem is forbidden in this branch.

## 3. Strict transform of a gap module is not automatically the new gap

A module supported on a Cartier centre can have zero strict transform under an identity blowup, while the ideal descent problem is unchanged. One must transform the whole exactified descent-interval diagram, not only `Q_-` and `Q_+`.

## 4. A single trace or conductor ideal is not the descent interval

The lower ideal `I_-` and upper ideal `I_+` control different containments. Either one alone can vanish or be too large while the upper ideal still fails to descend. Both quotients and the evaluation map are necessary.

## 5. The Ferrand norm ideal is not the complete coloured state

For the split cover `Y=X disjoint-union X` with ideals `(J_1,J_2)`, the norm shadow is `J_1J_2`. It forgets the ordered branch, owner and source labels. The norm ideal supplies a common base modification; it does not replace hereditary colour data.

## 6. Norm image formation is unsafe under nonflat base change

The Ferrand norm functor commutes with arbitrary base change, but the image of `Norm_f(J)->O_X` need not commute with a nonflat blowup. The source, target, kernel and cokernel of the norm map must be exactified.

## 7. Normalized blowup is not an allowed final step

The Norm--Rees shadow is a canonical comparison modification. The final algorithm must dominate it by a finite sequence of ordinary blowups in actual regular jointly legal centres.

## 8. Product principalization does not erase branch semantics

Even when a norm or orbit product becomes invertible, one must still know which branch remains singular and how source, boundary, passive and contact data transform. Complete all-chart reentry remains indispensable.

## 9. Local diagonal computations do not globalize without a toroidal chart groupoid

The formula `x_i=u_i^(e_i)` may fail outside a toroidal or monomial presentation. A global norm ideal exists, but the equality of its normalized pullback with the upper Newton modification is not proved in a general wild chart.

## 10. Alteration is diagnostic, not the final resolution

A regular or monomial upper alteration does not itself resolve the original scheme by ordinary birational blowups. It is used to read finite wild data and construct the base norm shadow.

## 11. Balanced flat-kill requires a schematically dense good open

A finite flat module may be supported on an entire component. Vanishing on an open which misses that component does not force zero. Every component of the prepared base must meet the descent-good open.

## 12. Truth boundary

The global norm ideal and toroidal comparison are closed. Balanced-gap elimination is conditional on simultaneous exactified flatification and X044 regular-centre realization. Event-Visible Wild Toroidalization, UDE-EV, the class-V ordinal, global termination, and general positive-characteristic resolution remain open.
