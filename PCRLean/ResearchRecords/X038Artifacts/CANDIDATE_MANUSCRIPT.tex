\begin{abstract}
We isolate a finite obstruction to the interchange of ambient strict transform
and normal associated grading along a regular carrier.  On every affine chart
of a centre-enriched trace blowup, the old chart-pulled graded piece and the new
normal graded piece, after its Cartier twist, map epimorphically to a canonical
image-filtration core.  The two kernels are respectively a Tor/base-change
defect and a power-saturation defect of Valabrega type.  Their simultaneous
vanishing gives the interchange isomorphism without a preferred comparison
map.  We formulate a lower-dimensional flat-kill compiler that enrolls these
kernels with the projective-normal passive portfolio.  The scheme-level
compiler and the positive-characteristic resolution theorem remain open.
\end{abstract}

\section{The affine common core}
Let $A$ be Noetherian, let $I\subset K\subset A$ be ideals, let $M$ be a
finite $A$-module, and choose $q\in K$.  Put
\[
 B=A[K/q],\qquad N=M\otimes_A B,
 \qquad Q_q(P)=P/H^0_{(q)}(P).
\]
For $a\geq0$ define
\[
 F_a=\operatorname{im}(I^aM\otimes_A B\longrightarrow N),\quad
 \overline G_a=F_a/F_{a+1},\quad
 G_a=(I^aM/I^{a+1}M)\otimes_A B,
\]
and set $C_a=Q_q(\overline G_a)$.

\begin{definition}[Relative power saturation]
For $P\subset N$, put
\[
 \operatorname{Sat}_q(P;N)=
 \{x\in N:\ q^n x\in P\text{ for some }n\geq0\}.
\]
Then
\[
 Q_q(N/P)\cong N/\operatorname{Sat}_q(P;N).
\]
\end{definition}

There is a canonical epimorphism
\[
 R_a:Q_q(G_a)\twoheadrightarrow C_a.
\]
Its kernel is the Tor/base-change defect.  Let $L$ denote the transformed
carrier ideal on the $q$-chart, so that $IB=qL$ after removal of chart power
torsion.  Multiplication by $q^a$ induces a second epimorphism
\[
 L_a:
 \bigl(L^aQ_q(N)/L^{a+1}Q_q(N)\bigr)\otimes\mathcal O(-aE)
 \twoheadrightarrow C_a.
\]
Its kernel is the Valabrega saturation defect.

\begin{proposition}[Common-core compiler]
If $\ker L_a=0=\ker R_a$, then there is a unique isomorphism between the two
sources which makes the triangle through $C_a$ commute.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Both epimorphisms are then isomorphisms.  Compose $L_a$ with the inverse of
$R_a$.
\end{proof}

\section{Finite mixed-Rees packet}
The expected global construction begins with the finite bigraded module
\[
 \mathcal R_{I,K}(M)=
 \bigoplus_{a,b\geq0}I^aK^bM\,u^av^b
\]
over the two-ideal Rees algebra.  Its finite kernels, exceptional-power torsion,
and relative Serre tail are predicted to sheafify to a coherent
Tor--Valabrega packet on the projectivized normal carrier.  Establishing the
exact chart identifications, overlap cocycles, and smooth-base-change law is a
load-bearing open theorem.

\section{Flat-kill legalization}
Let $C$ be a regular carrier and let $\mathcal T$ be the direct sum of the
projective-normal legality packet and the Tor--Valabrega packet.  On the open
locus where the proposed centre is already legal, $\mathcal T$ is zero.  An
admissible flatifying blowup makes its strict transform finitely presented and
flat.  If the good open meets every irreducible component, the flat transform
of the generically zero defect is zero.

The flatifier centre need not be regular.  Its source ideal is therefore
principalized on $C$, and every regular trace subcentre is recursively
legalized before ambient blowup.  Since each such subcentre has strictly
smaller carrier dimension, this is a well-founded nested recursion.

\begin{theorem}[Maximum-likelihood decisive theorem]
Assume the strong relative principalization and legalization program for
regular carriers of dimension strictly less than $d$.  Let $C$ be a regular
$d$-dimensional carrier in a smooth ambient scheme, equipped with a finite
source-labelled active, passive, logarithmic, and contact portfolio.  Then the
projective-normal and Tor--Valabrega packets admit a finite centre-enriched
flat-kill word in ordinary ambient blowups with regular, recursively legalized
trace centres.  After the word, the transformed carrier is jointly legal and
the Tor--Valabrega common-core maps are isomorphisms on every chart and
overlap.  All source, owner, boundary, and debt identities reenter without
reset.
\end{theorem}

\begin{remark}
The preceding theorem is a target statement.  It has not been proved.  In
particular, mixed-Rees finite presentation in the required scheme interface,
regular ambient realization of the flatifier, and hereditary no-reset remain
open.
\end{remark}
