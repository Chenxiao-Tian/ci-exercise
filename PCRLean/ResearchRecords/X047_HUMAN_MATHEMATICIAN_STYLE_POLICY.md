# X047 Human-Mathematician Style Policy

Future public manuscripts preserve all mathematical hypotheses, definitions, formulae, and dependency relations, but use the most likely prose of a working research mathematician.

- State the obstruction before introducing the construction.
- Explain why each hypothesis is needed at the point where it is used.
- Prefer ordinary definitions, lemmas, propositions, theorems, and proofs to status summaries.
- Use coordinates only for the local proof, and state the result intrinsically.
- Separate standard algebraic geometry from the genuinely new positive-characteristic step.
- Keep counterexamples near the theorem boundary they justify.
- Avoid repeated roadmaps, promotional adjectives, proof-management vocabulary, and software/process language.
- Retain technical terms such as Frobenius--Hasse state, mixed-Rees module, event complex, Amitsur obstruction, and source filtration only after they have been mathematically defined.
