# Projected series abstract

We construct a functorial embedded resolution algorithm for reduced schemes of
finite type over a perfect field of positive characteristic.  A marked
singularity is encoded by a finite intrinsic Frobenius-Hasse state carrying
active and passive owners, an ordered boundary, and fixed-ancestry source and
debt data.  Its affine conormal equations define a cokernel obstruction whose
content and projectivity discriminants yield graph, hybrid, unit,
Schur-Fitting, and radicial chambers.

Nonunique permissible closures are retained as a finite intersection
arrangement and serialized by a symmetry-compatible wonderful building set.
Regular-support nonreduced intersections are represented by anchor-contact
ideals on regular lower-dimensional carriers.  Passive normal flatness is
represented by finite projective-normal packets on projectivized normal
bundles.

The central transform formalism uses source-labelled carrier ideals.  A
carrier ideal is principalized in lower dimension and lifted to the ambient
scheme together with its centre data; residual Cartier centres are retained as
ambient trace steps even when their carrier blowups are identities.  An
extended bifiltered Rees module compares ambient strict transform followed by
normal associated grading with projective-normal pure transform followed by
base change.  The kernel and cokernel form a finite Rees interchange defect.
Vanishing gives exact passive/contact transport, while nonvanishing produces a
strict Fitting/support recursion.

All standard charts and overlaps preserve the complete finite state without
reset.  A composite well-founded rank eliminates Rees interchange defects,
contact, births, exceptional recharge, kangaroo recurrence, singular Fitting
kernels, and immediate defect.  Descent and symmetry-compatible finite
serialization produce a global sequence of ordinary blowups, yielding
principalization, embedded resolution, and nonembedded resolution.

The preceding abstract is a maximum-likelihood reconstruction of the eventual
series, not a claim of completion.
