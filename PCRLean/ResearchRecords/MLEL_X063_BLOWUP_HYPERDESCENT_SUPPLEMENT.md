# MLEL-X063 Supplement

## Complete Blowup-Square Hyperdescent and Factorization-Invariant Event Cycles

### 1. One-step square

For a regular blowup `b:W'->W` with centre `C` and exceptional divisor `E`, the complete datum is not merely `Rb_*K_(W')`. It consists of the `W'`-, `C`-, and `E`-objects, the compatibility isomorphism on `E`, the two open--closed recollement triangles, all connecting maps, and the complete algebraic labels.

The standard abstract blowup-square triangle reconstructs the underlying torsion etale complex. Universal exactification extends this reconstruction to the finite system of Rees multiplications, Hasse operators, Tor--Valabrega comparisons, Fitting layers, and labelled filtrations used by the resolution state.

### 2. Iteration

A finite centre word produces a finite cube. A face is indexed by the choices of open or exceptional branch at each blowup together with ordered exceptional intersections. The total homotopy fibre reconstructs the source state. The source filtration identifies chart aliases before the event multiset is formed.

### 3. Neutral changes of factorization

- The blowup of an invertible ideal is an isomorphism and contributes an identity cone.
- Blowups of disjoint centres commute; their two squares form the same Cartesian 2-cube.
- A hereditary common refinement pulls both systems to one finite cube. New faces are either contractible copies or lie over the refinement centre and therefore have lower carrier dimension or proper support.

Hence the essential nondegenerate event cycle is invariant.

### 4. Strict alternative

At a maximal essential face, algebraic--constructible comparison identifies the leading perverse quotient with the coherent Cartier--Rees quotient enrolled in the actual-centre portfolio. Universal exactification makes the successor generically epimorphic. A nonzero kernel lowers finite length. If the generic quotient disappears but the event remains nonzero, blowup-square conservativity places it on a proper exceptional face. Thus every new token is strictly below a deleted maximal token.

### 5. Boundary of the statement

The theorem is a finite, labelled, centre-exact hyperdescent statement. It does not assert unrestricted descent for all objects in an arbitrary topology, nor does it use normalization, weighted blowups, or stacky modifications as final resolution steps.
