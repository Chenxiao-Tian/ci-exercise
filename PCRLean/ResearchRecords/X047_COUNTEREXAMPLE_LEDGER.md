# X047 Counterexample and Failure-Boundary Ledger

## 1. Group stability is not fpqc descent

Let `A=k[t]`, `B=k[s]`, `t=s^2`, in characteristic not two. The ideal `(s)` is stable under `s -> -s`, but it is not extended from an ideal of `A`. The finite cover is ramified and is not a torsor over the branch point. A finite-group orbit test is therefore insufficient.

## 2. Trace zero is not descent

A vanishing trace comparison says nothing by itself about equality of the two pullbacks on `Y x_X Y`. Descent requires a comparison isomorphism compatible with the ideal embedding and a cocycle on the triple fibre product.

## 3. Differential shadow zero is not descent

Kahler differentials detect ramification and defect features of a valued extension, not effectiveness of an arbitrary upper centre ideal or Rees algebra. A zero differential module or annihilator condition cannot replace the Cech datum.

## 4. A descended module need not descend a chosen submodule automatically

Even when an ambient module is pulled back from the base, a submodule selected upstairs may not descend. The inclusion of the submodule into the ambient module must be part of the descent datum.

## 5. Descent of an ideal is not descent of a centre word unless the Rees structure is retained

The blowup is determined by the graded Rees algebra. Stagewise centre ideals, their powers, the grading, and the centre-exact source semantics must all descend. An isomorphic final modification does not recover the strict-transform word.

## 6. Zero on the double overlap without cocycle is insufficient

An isomorphism `p_0^*Q ~= p_1^*Q` on the double fibre product need not be a descent datum unless its two composites agree on the triple fibre product. The cocycle defect is an independent finite coherent shadow.

## 7. Flattening is not yet a regular-centre word

Flattening by blowup may use a singular centre. The X047 zero-shadow theorem assumes the flattening modification has been realized by the X044 lower-dimensional actual-centre block or replaced by a centre-exact common refinement. It does not promote arbitrary flatification to an allowed final centre.

## 8. Upper regularity without descent data does not regularize the base

A regular upper centre on a finite cover descends only when it is the pullback of a downstairs centre. Once a finite faithfully flat descent datum exists, regularity is reflected; without it, there may be no downstairs centre at all.

## 9. Zero typed ramification shadows do not imply the complete shadow is zero

The complete shadow includes Cech comparison, cocycle, algebra multiplication, ideal embeddings, source labels, boundary certificates, and the terminal packet. X046's informal zero-shadow proof is valid only after this enlargement.

## 10. A finite upper word is an additional hypothesis

X047 proves descent of a given finite compatible upper word. It does not prove that local uniformization after alteration monomializes the entire event-visible packet by a finite word satisfying Cech compatibility. That is CCWM.

## 11. Alteration remains diagnostic

The finite flat or uniformizing alteration is not the final resolution of the original model. The output of the zero-shadow theorem is a descended word of ordinary blowups on the original base.

## 12. Truth boundary

Zero-shadow exact descent is closed in the stated finite-flat chamber. Cech-compatible wild monomialization, strict reduction of every nonzero shadow, UDE-EV, the class-V ordinal, global termination, and general positive-characteristic resolution remain open.
