\begin{abstract}
We isolate the hereditary mechanism required to transport passive normal
flatness through centre-enriched blowup words.  Purification before and after
associated grading differs by exceptional power torsion; once the purified
associated-graded module is flat over a regular carrier, this torsion vanishes.
For a nested regular flag, Hironaka's tensor decomposition makes normal
flatness transitive.  Consequently, after one carrier has been legalized,
passive flatness and exceptional torsion do not recharge on subsequent regular
subcarriers.  The remaining decisive problem is an all-chart regular-flag
lifting theorem comparing ambient strict transforms with carrier-side Rees
transforms, including the exceptional grading twist and hereditary source data.
The global resolution theorem remains open.
\end{abstract}

\section{Purification and filter regularity}
Let $A$ be Noetherian, let $L\subset A$ be an ideal, let $N$ be finite, and let
$u\in A$ be the equation of an exceptional Cartier divisor.  Put
\[
 T=H^0_{(u)}(N),\qquad
 G=\operatorname{gr}_L(N),\qquad
 H=\operatorname{gr}^{\mathrm{ind}}_L(T),\qquad
 U=H^0_{(u)}(G).
\]
The purification--grading exact sequence has the candidate form
\[
 0\longrightarrow U/H
 \longrightarrow \operatorname{gr}_L(N/T)
 \longrightarrow G/U\longrightarrow0,
\]
and
\[
 U/H=H^0_{(u)}\bigl(\operatorname{gr}_L(N/T)\bigr).
\]
Thus the discrepancy is not an arbitrary transform defect; it is the failure
of $u$ to be filter regular on the purified associated graded.

\begin{proposition}[flat filter regularity]
Let $B$ be a commutative ring, let $P$ be a flat $B$-module, and let $u$ be a
nonzerodivisor of $B$.  Then $H^0_{(u)}(P)=0$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Tensoring the injection $B\xrightarrow{u}B$ with $P$ preserves injectivity.
Hence multiplication by $u$ on $P$ is injective.  Induction gives that
$u^m p=0$ implies $p=0$ for every $m\geq1$.
\end{proof}

For an SNC exceptional divisor with local equations $u_1,\ldots,u_r$, every
exceptional monomial $u^\alpha$ is a nonzerodivisor on the regular carrier.
One carrier-flatness certificate therefore eliminates the purification defects
of every owner and every boundary stratum simultaneously.

\section{Hironaka's tensor comparison}
Let $R$ be Noetherian and let $I\subset J\subset R$.  Write
\[
 \overline R=R/I,\qquad \overline J=J/I,\qquad K=R/J.
\]
Assume that $\overline J$ is a complete-intersection ideal of $\overline R$.
For a finite $R$-module $M$ there is a natural graded comparison
\[
 \Phi_{I,J,M}:\bigl(\operatorname{gr}_I(M)/J\operatorname{gr}_I(M)\bigr)
 \otimes_K \operatorname{gr}_{\overline J}(\overline R)
 \longrightarrow \operatorname{gr}_J(M).
\]
The module version of Hironaka's theorem asserts that $\Phi_{I,J,M}$ is an
isomorphism when $\operatorname{gr}_I(M)$ is free over $\overline R$.
Furthermore,
\[
 \operatorname{gr}_{\overline J}(\overline R)
 \cong \operatorname{Sym}_K(\overline J/\overline J^2).
\]

\begin{corollary}[normal-flatness transitivity]
Let $D\subset C\subset X$ be regular immersions and let $M$ be coherent.  If
$M$ is normally flat along $C$, then $M$ is normally flat along $D$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}[Candidate proof interface]
Locally set $I=I_C$ and $J=I_D$.  The relative ideal $J/I$ is generated by a
regular sequence.  Hironaka's comparison identifies $\operatorname{gr}_J(M)$
with the tensor product of the restriction of $\operatorname{gr}_I(M)$ to $D$
and the symmetric relative normal algebra.  Both factors are flat over $D$.
The exact sheafified finite-owner statement and its base-change interface are
still to be supplied in the project.
\end{proof}

\section{Regular-flag charts}
Etale-locally a regular flag admits coordinates
\[
 I_C=(x_1,\ldots,x_r),\qquad
 I_D=(x_1,\ldots,x_r,y_1,\ldots,y_s).
\]
In the blowup of $D$, only the $y_j$-pivot charts meet the strict transform of
$C$.  On the $y_1$-chart,
\[
 x_i=y_1x_i',\qquad y_j=y_1y_j'\ (j>1),
 \qquad I_{C'}=(x_1',\ldots,x_r').
\]
The controlled carrier ideal is already saturated in this coordinate chamber,
and the conormal generators differ by the common exceptional factor $y_1$.
This determines a canonical grading line twist.

\section{The regular-flag flat-lift theorem}
\begin{theorem}[maximum-likelihood edge theorem]
Let $D\subset C\subset X$ be a regular flag in a regular Noetherian ambient
scheme.  Assume that $D$ is jointly legal for the complete active, passive,
Rees-comparison, logarithmic, source, contact, and debt portfolio attached to
$C$.  After blowing up $X$ in $D$, let $C'$ and $M'$ be the strict transforms.
Then on every relevant chart and overlap there is a canonical graded
isomorphism
\[
 \operatorname{gr}_{C'}(M')
 \cong
 \operatorname{ST}_D\bigl(\operatorname{gr}_C(M)\bigr)
 \otimes \mathcal O(E)^{\bullet},
\]
with the sign and degree convention fixed by the chart equation
$x_i=y_1x_i'$.  The isomorphism is compatible with owner sums, smooth base
change, boundary strata, source labels, and controlled marks.  If the
carrier-side transform is flat, the purification--grading defect vanishes and
normal flatness persists down every later regular subflag.
\end{theorem}

\begin{remark}
This theorem is a target statement, not an established result.  The local
coordinate ideal calculation is standard; the complete Rees-module map,
finite exceptional kernel/cokernel packet, overlap equality, and hereditary
ledger transport remain open.
\end{remark}

\section{One flatifier and dimension-strict recursion}
The proposed legalization macro is
\[
 \begin{array}{c}
 \text{raw regular carrier }C\\
 \downarrow\\
 \text{one centre-enriched admissible flatifier}\\
 \downarrow\\
 \text{regular source-labelled word on }C\\
 \downarrow\\
 \text{recursive legalization of every }D\subset C,\ \dim D<\dim C\\
 \downarrow\\
 \text{ambient trace blowups and regular-flag flat lifts}\\
 \downarrow\\
 \text{flat carrier with no passive or exceptional recharge.}
 \end{array}
\]
No repeated same-dimensional re-flatification is permitted.  The nested rank
orders carrier dimension first, then the remaining immutable word height, then
Rees defect, contact, and debt.
