## 1. Refinement of the public manuscript

The final X046 manuscript replaces an arbitrary prime-degree tower in the defect-carrying separable part by a sharper tame-wild reduction.

After henselization and a finite defectless tame base refinement, the separable extension carrying the defect is dominated by a finite purely wild Galois extension. Its Galois group is a finite p-group and therefore admits a normal central series with cyclic quotients of order p. The purely inseparable part is separately refined into radicial degree-p layers.

Thus the event-visible defect packet is represented by a finite typed tower whose nontrivial defect-carrying layers are:

```text
cyclic separable degree-p wild layers;
radicial degree-p layers;
defectless tame comparison layers.
```

Defect is multiplicative in the tower; any nontrivial total defect is witnessed in a wild or radicial degree-p layer. In equal characteristic p, the cyclic separable layers are Artin--Schreier layers.

## 2. Why the refinement matters

- It avoids importing irrelevant prime-to-p layers into the class-V rank.
- Dependent/independent classification is applied only to the degree-p wild layers where it is defined.
- The differential-ramification portfolio is indexed by typed wild layers rather than by an arbitrary field composition series.
- Different choices are compared after a finite common tame refinement. The rank uses the multiset of typed wild-layer profiles, not the length of one selected tower.

## 3. Truth boundary

This is a valuation-theoretic organization of the finite extension packet. It does not prove Independent-Defect Effectivity, produce an ordinary regular centre on the original model, or establish global termination. The public manuscript's IDE theorem remains a maximum-likelihood effectivity/descent statement.
