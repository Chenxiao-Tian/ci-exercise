# X046 Counterexample and Firewall Ledger

## 1. Fixed projection defect does not decrease under model blowup

For a fixed finite valued-field extension and fixed valuation extension, the degree, ramification index, residue degree and defect factor are field-theoretic. Birational changes of the chosen models do not turn the defect factor into a stage counter. Any proposed rank using “remaining defect” for a fixed projection is rejected.

## 2. Cofinal approximation depth is not intrinsic

A valuation pro-object is isomorphic to every cofinal tail. The number of key polynomials or approximants already consumed, one selected tail index, or completion depth cannot be a strict invariant of the pro-isomorphism class.

## 3. Finite constructible observations are eventually constant

Along a constructibly convergent immediate-defect approximation tail, every invariant factoring through a fixed finite constructible partition is eventually constant. A uniform escape must act on a quasi-compact valuation neighbourhood, not on a chosen approximant.

## 4. Exact defect support may be noncoherent

A nonprincipal ramification cut in a valuation ring cannot be the extension of a finitely generated ideal from a Noetherian model, because finitely generated ideals in a valuation ring are principal. X046 records only event-visible coherent shadows; it never identifies the full cut with one coherent centre.

## 5. Ramification ideal alone is not complete

In higher degree, nontrivial defect need not force a nonprincipal ramification ideal. A criterion based on one ramification ideal can miss a defect tower. X046 first refines to prime layers and retains differential, trace, conductor and coefficient shadows together.

## 6. Alteration is not resolution of the original model

Local uniformization after a finite extension or purely inseparable alteration does not by itself produce a projective birational resolution of the original variety. The uniformizing alteration is diagnostic until an alteration-shadow effectivity theorem produces ordinary centres downstairs.

## 7. Norm/product compensation

A product or norm of several layer shadows may be principal or a unit even though one individual layer comparison is nontrivial. Layer colours are kept separate and composed by the shadow octahedron. No product ideal replaces typed layer semantics.

## 8. Trace splitting can fail in residue characteristic p

For a separable prime-degree layer of degree `p`, normalized trace need not split the unit map. Independent Artin--Schreier defect therefore cannot be treated as a prime-to-p finite cover. Kähler differentials, ramification and coefficient shadows are required.

## 9. Dependent defect is not automatically geometrically resolved

The valuation classification connects a dependent Artin--Schreier defect extension with a purely inseparable immediate extension. It does not automatically construct a regular permissible centre on the original model. The dependent layer is routed to the existing radicial actual-centre theorem, whose geometric hypotheses remain load bearing.

## 10. Stable coherent shadows do not automatically imply terminality

Noetherian ACC only proves that visible coherent shadows eventually stabilize. The decisive IDE statement must show that a stable independent-defect packet which remains nonterminal has a nonzero effective shadow or that the monomial alteration packet descends. Without this semantic-completeness theorem, stabilization is not escape.

## 11. New extension models are not free sources

Passing to a larger normal closure, refining a prime tower or replacing one common model by another does not create a new source token. The valuation/projection ancestry is immutable, and factorization refinements are compared in one common tower.

## 12. Truth boundary

Finite shadow hulls, prime-layer reduction, alteration portfolios and shadow octahedra are standard or finite constructions. Independent-Defect Effectivity, UDE-EV, the strict class-V ordinal, full global termination and general positive-characteristic resolution remain open.
