## 1. First red-team correction: regular quotient is not a legal-centre theorem

For a finite-dimensional vector space `V` over a field and any finite linear
packet, the ideal generated by the common linear kernel has a regular
symmetric-algebra quotient. This does **not** yet produce a legal blowup centre.
A legal affine centre must satisfy

```text
ideal != bottom   -- the closed centre is not the whole ambient space
ideal != top      -- the closed centre is not empty
```

as well as marked permissibility, singular-locus containment, boundary
transversality, passive safety, and hereditary transform laws.

The identity packet is the minimal falsifier:

```text
packet map  = id
kernel      = 0
centre ideal = bottom
quotient     = regular
```

Thus

```text
regular quotient  -/->  legal nonwhole centre.
```

The exact expected boundary is

```text
centreIdeal(packet) = bottom  <->  packetMap is injective.
```

## 2. Second red-team correction: strictness is not marked permissibility

The linear ideal `(X)` is nonzero and proper, but the mark-two packet generated
by `X` is not permissible for `(X)` because

```text
X notin (X)^2.
```

Hence the proof compiler must retain separate gates for

```text
regularity
strictness
marked-power containment
singular-locus containment
owner/passive/boundary legality.
```

No one gate may be inferred silently from another.

## 3. Concrete examples and the hybrid discovery

### E1. `y^p - x^(p+1)`

For the reduced cusp in characteristic `p`, the ambient Jacobian derivatives
are

```text
d/dy = 0
d/dx = -x^p.
```

The hypersurface singular locus is not merely `x = 0`: after adjoining the
equation and reducing, it is the origin `(x,y)`. The ordinary derivative defect
supplies `x`, while the invisible pure Frobenius term supplies the root `y`.
The marked equation satisfies

```text
y^p - x^(p+1) in (x,y)^p.
```

Therefore the correct centre is the **hybrid sum**

```text
J = J_defect + J_root = (x,y),
```

not a disjoint choice between `(x)` and `(y)`.

This example falsifies the exclusivity built into `PKRE-001`.

### E2. A pure prime-power form `L^(p^e)`

The ordinary polar packet may vanish or become unhelpfully injective, but the
marked equation has the actual root hyperplane `(L)` and lies in `(L)^(p^e)`.
The hybrid construction reduces to the root component.

### E3. A genuinely separable ordinary defect

When no Frobenius-invisible component remains, the hybrid construction reduces
to the strict kernel/differential component.

### E4. Zero packet

A zero packet has full common kernel and cuts out the linear origin. This may be
correct at an isolated maximal point, but it cannot by itself choose a coherent
centre on a nonisolated singular locus. It exposes singular-locus containment
and globalization obligations.

### E5. Full separating packet

A packet separating every direction has bottom centre ideal. Such a packet
cannot trigger an ordinary blowup in the whole ambient space. It must trigger a
root extraction, a lower Hasse defect, a terminality theorem, or a strict
refinement/escape.

### E6. Several owners

Each owner may have a nonzero legal centre while the naive union packet is
injective. Therefore owner aggregation can destroy the centre. The correct
fallback may be a finite owner-safe centre word or an obstruction-guided
intersection/sum construction rather than the kernel of the union packet.

## 4. Candidate hybrid object

Let `Sigma` be a prepared Hasse-saturated marked/Rees state and let

```text
E_Sigma : V_Sigma -> Q_Sigma
```

be its finite intrinsic packet-evaluation morphism. Define

```text
K_Sigma = span(iota(ker E_Sigma)).
```

Let `R_Sigma` be the actual ideal generated by all certified prime-power roots
of the Frobenius-invisible marked layers after permitted exceptional cleaning.
Let `D_Sigma` be the actual defect/Fitting ideal extracted from the lowest
non-Frobenius Hasse layer. The candidate centre is

```text
J_Sigma = K_Sigma + R_Sigma + D_Sigma,
```

followed by a regular-core/Fitting compiler if the sum is not regular.

This sum is not automatically permissible or regular. The central bridge must
prove exactly when a sub-sum or a finite centre word is legal.

## 5. Revised branch architecture

### H. Hybrid-centre branch

Construct a finite family of actual components

```text
K_Sigma, R_Sigma, D_Sigma, B_Sigma
```

where `B_Sigma` is any boundary correction required for SNC. Produce either a
single actual ideal

```text
J_Sigma = K_Sigma + R_Sigma + D_Sigma + B_Sigma
```

or a finite regular centre word. Certify separately:

```text
bottom < J_Sigma < top
marked permissibility for every active owner
normal flatness and Tor safety for every passive owner
singular-locus containment
boundary SNC
all-chart transform
hereditary no-reset.
```

### E. Escape/refinement branch

If no legal hybrid centre exists, construct one of:

1. a lower-order Hasse defect;
2. a Fitting-rank drop and a new determinant chart;
3. an earlier strict Hilbert-Samuel/order/dimension drop;
4. an owner-conflict obstruction producing a finite centre word;
5. a higher-Frobenius/radicial packet in the rank-zero chamber;
6. a finite obstruction class showing why the current components do not glue.

The escape must strictly decrease a declared packet/Fitting/history rank.

The architecture is therefore no longer an exclusive three-way partition. It
is a two-level compiler:

```text
component extraction
  -> legal hybrid centre or finite centre word
  -> otherwise strict escape/refinement.
```

## 6. Strong bridge conjecture

### CONJ-PKHRE-002

For every nonterminal prepared state over a perfect field of positive
characteristic, finite intrinsic packetization and finite Hasse saturation
produce, on a finite principal-open atlas, a finite family of coherent actual
components

```text

Frobenius-root component
ordinary/Hasse-defect component
boundary correction component.
```

These components admit one of two certified outcomes:

1. a legal actual regular hybrid centre or finite centre word; or
2. a finite escape/refinement macro with a strict earlier geometric or causal
   rank drop.

All presentation changes transport the component family by exact ideal
transport, and the classification cannot reset after cleaning, saturation,
normalization, or integral closure.

This conjecture is substantially stronger than the currently proved linear
algebra and may require additional preparation, owner, or normal-flatness
hypotheses.

## 7. Minimal falsifiers

Reject or weaken the conjecture if any of the following occurs:

- an injective packet with no root component and no strict lower Hasse/Fitting
  defect;
- a strict hybrid ideal not contained in the marked singular locus;
- a component sum that destroys regularity with no finite regular-core word;
- a strict regular component sum that fails marked-power containment;
- two equivalent packets with nonisomorphic hereditary component transforms;
- a passive Tor witness destroyed by the hybrid centre;
- a side chart in which all components vanish without a rank drop;
- a quasilinear rank-zero family whose root components do not glue;
- an infinite sequence of refinements with no finite Noetherian witness.
